GENERATION 03 · 26 OCTOBER 2026

A complete run.
A reason to move.

Surge now has five 30-second encounters followed by a final battle. Within each round the field evolves through fixed Barkley reaction–diffusion equations. Between rounds all excitation, recovery, effects and pending warnings are cleared. Changes to the game come from excitation geometry, source timing, cutter choices and objectives. Bloom drift has been removed from the main game.

One health point is restored at each encounter transition and for each emitter disabled, up to three. Surviving alone does not finish the final battle. Survival points stop increasing at the final battle; the completion bonus rewards quicker source removal and remaining health.

Difficulty and power-ups

Easy offers a power-up every 10 seconds of run time, Medium every 30, and Hard every 60. Choice screens pause both timers. The first choice selects a cutter, the second refines it. All subsequent choices stack faster cycling (12%), extra reach (4 units) or extra width (1 unit) and repair one health point. Limits are 0.09 seconds minimum between pulses, 50 reach and 12 width. Power-ups continue in the final battle. Records are separate per difficulty.

Three cutters, three refinements

Lance has a long, narrow beam. Fan trades range for width. Double pulse fires twice in quick succession, followed by a longer recovery. At the second upgrade, choose faster cycling, extra reach with reduced width, or extra width with reduced beam length. All nine specialised combinations preserve a gap between the player and the suppression beam, preventing a permanent shield under the ship.

What remains to playtest

Difficulty and enjoyment still require human feedback. Simple idle and repetitive movement policies fail early; that does not establish that the opening is well balanced. The standalone boss test establishes that the objective can be completed through ordinary game inputs, not that the full scored run is easy or optimally paced.

The boss layout is fixed, so repeated seed values are not independent boss scenarios. The numerical solver is deliberately coarse and has not undergone convergence verification. Touch controls are implemented but have not been tested on a physical phone or tablet.

Practice and records

Practice offers unlimited lives, direct access to every encounter and the initial cutter-choice screens. It never changes the personal-best record. The game stores the best score locally in your browser, with no external analytics added by the game. Sound effects are optional and start muted.

Model

Barkley excitable medium on a 160 × 104 grid. Fixed a = 0.75, b = 0.025, epsilon = 0.04, activator diffusion 1, spatial step 1, Euler step 0.025. Four chemical steps advance per 60 Hz game tick. Outer grid edges are absorbing. The cutter clears excitation and raises recovery to at least 0.9 within its beam. This is an excitable reaction–diffusion game, rather than a classical stationary Turing pattern.
